# Decisions

Every choice that shaped the build: what was picked, what it was picked over, and why. Newest last. Numbers come from the runs in [`evidence/`](../evidence/).

| # | Date | Decision | Alternative | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2026-11-22 | **Rebuild wind/solar history from AESO metered volumes** (99 wind and solar assets, summed per hour) | Impute from the forecast CSV, or train without the regressor | The CSV only holds about 2 days of actuals. Metered volumes cover 2026-01-01 to about T-3 days, leaving only **10 imputed hours** out of 7,979 (the DST hour and a 9-hour seam before the CSV starts). |
| 2 | 2026-11-22 | **Key bronze tables on `begin_datetime_utc`**, keep `*_mpt` naive columns for display | Key on MPT | MPT repeats an hour when daylight saving time ends, so it is not unique. The session time zone is pinned to UTC so naive MPT values are stored exactly as AESO publishes them. |
| 3 | 2026-11-22 | **Ingest notebooks MERGE into bronze; a Lakeflow declarative pipeline builds silver/gold** | One big notebook, or everything in the pipeline | The AESO API needs Python and a secret, which suits notebooks. Cleaning, joining and expectations suit declarative materialized views, which give lineage and data-quality metrics for free. |
| 4 | 2026-11-22 | **Use the metastore's governed tags** (`domain=operations`, `layer`, `classification=public`, `data_product`) | Free-form tags (`domain=power_market`) | The first run failed: `domain` is a governed tag with a fixed value list. Fitting the customer's tag policies is the governance story; free-form `subject_area` carries the detail. |
| 5 | 2026-11-22 | **AESO key as a Unity Catalog secret**, read with `dbutils.secrets.get(catalog=, schema=, key=)`; govern it through the UC REST API | Workspace secret scope; or `COMMENT ON SECRET` in SQL | One governance model for data and credentials, audited in `system.access.audit`. Secret DDL is not enabled in Spark SQL here, so the comment and grant check go through REST. The app's service principal **is** granted `READ SECRET` (owner decision on 2026-11-22, so the app identity can call AESO directly later); the build first proposed withholding it because the app does not call AESO today. |
| 6 | 2026-11-22 | **Two converged models feed the ensemble**, not the originals | Average the original Prophet and Monte Carlo | The originals disagree by 24.5 $/MWh on average over the next 24 hours in the latest run. After convergence (Prophet without Spike hours and a 50x tighter changepoint prior; Monte Carlo with per-regime hourly factors) the gap is 4.9. The original Prophet chases spikes: backtest MAE 34.9 vs 11.2 for the regime-aware Prophet. |
| 7 | 2026-11-22 | **Ensemble weight = probability the current regime holds after h hours, bounded [0.3, 0.7]** | Fixed 50/50 | Prophet is right when the regime is stable; Monte Carlo is right when a switch is likely. The weight moves from Prophet to Monte Carlo as the horizon grows. The bounds stop either model from being dropped. |
| 8 | 2026-11-22 | **MAPE skips hours under $1/MWh** | Plain MAPE | Alberta prices sit near $0 for long stretches; percentage error there is undefined or explodes and would hide the real ranking. MAE in $/MWh is the headline metric. |
| 9 | 2026-11-22 | **Benchmark against AESO's own forecast and same-hour-yesterday** | Compare models only with each other | A buyer asks "is this better than what I already get?". The ensemble (MAE 9.7) is 56% better than same-hour-yesterday (21.9). AESO's forecast (3.4) is better still, but it is published only hours ahead. We say so plainly rather than hide it. |
| 10 | 2026-11-22 | **Measure decision value, not only accuracy**: shift 4 h/day of flexible load by each strategy's ranking, settle at actual prices | Report MAE only | The best MAE is not the best decision. On the 5 backtest days the ensemble captured 64% of the perfect-foresight savings, while the regime-aware Prophet captured 94%, because it ranks the expensive hours better. The schedule signal should use the hour ranking, not the point forecast. |
| 11 | 2026-11-22 | **Flexible MW and shift hours are labelled assumptions**, reported per MW and at 5 / 10 / 20 MW | Pick one site's MW | We have no Enbridge load data and do not invent it. The real price facts (hours above $150 are 2.6% of hours but 35.8% of a flat load's cost) need no assumption. |
| 12 | 2026-11-22 | **Hourly forecast, daily backtest + registration** | Retrain and register every hour | The forecast must be fresh each hour. Registering 24 model versions a day adds noise; a daily backtest gives a stable, comparable `@champion`. |
| 13 | 2026-11-22 | **One Databricks Asset Bundle for every stage** | SDK scripts per stage | One `databricks bundle deploy` recreates jobs, pipeline, experiment, Lakebase, app and Genie space in any workspace. |
| 14 | 2026-11-22 | **Screen-aware "Analyze with Genie" links into Genie One** from every card, plus a curated Genie space | A custom chat panel in the app | No chat UI to build or maintain; people get the native, governed Genie experience with the question already typed from what is on screen. The curated space carries the instructions and scores 8/8 on the benchmark. |
| 15 | 2026-11-22 | **Hot cards from Lakebase synced tables, history cards from the SQL warehouse** | Everything through the warehouse, or everything synced | The cards people glance at every 5 minutes get millisecond Postgres reads; aggregations over 90 days stay in Delta, where they belong. Lakebase also gives the app a transactional table for ops actions. |
| 16 | 2026-11-22 | **App grants: schema and tables only; `USE CATALOG` inherited from `account users`** | Grant `USE CATALOG` to the app | The catalog is shared and we lack MANAGE on it. `scripts/grant_app_sp.py` checks the inherited grant and stops with the exact statement for an admin if it is ever missing. |
